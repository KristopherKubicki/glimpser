Findings and model notes

  • Multi-frame denoising: guarded fusion reduced noise in a synthetic static low-light test. Motion tests preserved newly arrived and departed subjects. Real low-light quality is still under review.
  • Square pixels: horizon leveling now scales both axes equally. Mismatched historical dimensions are rejected instead of stretched. Older derivatives are not retroactively corrected.
  • Fisheye inspection: inspection found lens curvature in the original. Neural sharpening does not calibrate the lens or straighten the doorway.
  • Motion: held frames mean interpolation was not used. Smoother playback is not proof of accurate reconstructed motion.
  • Low-light pilot: native neural restoration now has an optional three-scale shadow recovery and bounded local contrast stage. The saved Backyard comparison reveals more lawn and fence detail, but lens reflections remain. Lighting correction is not a new neural model or deglare reconstruction.
  • Jetty source quality: the October 14 dawn pilot contains app controls, a loading spinner and a tilted horizon. Its enhanced copy is rejected for display. Clean capture extraction matters more here than additional sharpening.
  • Multi-stage processing: aligned brightness/color fusion can feed neural restoration in the review queue. These derivatives are not automatically substituted in rotation.
  • Neural glare pilot: Flare7K++ Uformer is connected to the private Spark review pipeline. Overlapping tiles preserve camera dimensions; a bounded blend protects clipped light cores. It targets lens flare, not reflections through glass. Technical completion does not establish improvement. No automatic rotation. The first Backyard trial preserved geometry but left the prominent lens-edge reflections; it was rejected for rotation because no material improvement was visible. Upstream code and weights have noncommercial restrictions and are installed separately; store footage is outside this pilot.
  • New private review models: FastDVDnet denoises adjacent video frames, Retinexformer restores low-light stills, and SwinIR targets JPEG artifacts. All three have passed GPU execution checks. Results require camera-specific visual review and do not enter rotation automatically. The first Backyard low-light trial amplified artifacts; the JPEG trial softened fine detail. Both remain excluded from rotation. Temporal denoising preserves real frames; it does not interpolate missing moments. Glass-reflection removal still needs a separate model and license review. Fog and weather remain meaningful scene content.
  • CIDNet low-light trial: HVI-CIDNet is installed on the private Spark with pinned code and weights. The first clean Backyard night capture was monochrome; the output washed out contrast and lifted glare, so it was rejected. The recipe now refuses near-monochrome inputs before inference. A clean color-night trial also over-brightened the scene and amplified texture artifacts; it was rejected too. This experiment is excluded from automatic rotation.
